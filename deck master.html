02 Data · choice of series

Train on spot, trade on futures

The continuous futures series switches contract every month, and each switch shows up as a price jump that is not a return. We learn dynamics from the roll-free spot price and apply the forecasts to the contract we can actually trade.

① Training target FRED / EIA
Spot price
DCOILBRENTEU · DCOILWTICO
  • One physical price per day → no contract roll
  • Returns are true returns → clean target for estimation
  • Published with ~1 week delay (last obs 14 Oct vs 21 Oct)
② Trading & scoring Yahoo
Front-month futures
BZ=F (ICE Brent) · CL=F (NYMEX WTI)
  • Tradable, settles the same evening
  • Close 21 Oct: Brent 110.04 · WTI 98.07
  • Unadjusted rolls → jump = calendar spread
③ Predictors publication lag applied
Market · dailycrack spread, natgas, XLE, S&P 500, VIX, DXY, gold, copper, USD/CAD, USD/NOK, EEMt
Rates · FREDUST 10Y, 10Y–2Y, 10Y–3M, 10Y breakeven, OVX, EPUt+1
Weekly · EIAcrude inventories, Weekly Economic Indext+6
All entered as changes or 1-year z-scores, never raw levels.
Why not train on futures directly? A roll day adds the spread between two contracts to the “return”. In steep markets that is several percent, and the model would learn noise that no trader could earn.
Why still trade futures? Spot is a physical assessment, not an instrument. P&L, hit-rates and RMSE are scored on the futures price an investor would hold.
Hygiene: WTI history starts 2020-06-01 (skips the −$37.63 settle of 20 Apr 2020) · EIA curve-slope files end Apr 2024, feature dropped · FRED crude-stock series discontinued, sourced from EIA.

07 Model 1 · ARMA-GARCH · method

ARMA-GARCH: a model for the second moment

A low-order mean with fat-tailed, clustered volatility. We do not expect it to beat no change on the mean. Its job is to say how wide tomorrow’s range should be.

Specification MA(2) · GARCH(1,1)-t
rt = εt + θ1εt−1 + θ2εt−2
σ2t = ω + α ε2t−1 + β σ2t−1,  εt ~ Student-t(ν)
From 1-day to h-day
μ̂h = Σk≤h r̂t+k,   σh2 = Σk ck2 · Et[σ2t+k]
exact variance of the cumulative return; ck = summed MA impulse responses
  • Fitted on daily log returns; refit quarterly, filtered daily with fixed parameters
  • Low order, no exogenous inputs → nothing to overfit
Fitted parameters Oct 2026 refit
ParameterBrentWTIReading
θ1, θ2−0.019, −0.000−0.016, −0.027mean ≈ 0
α (news)0.0820.079shocks feed vol
β (memory)0.9130.908vol is sticky
α + β0.9950.987half-life ≈ 2–7 months
ν (tail)5.67.7fat tails
Role in the system: the ±1σ envelope in every chart, email and the trading rule’s noise filter.
Why Student-t: the EDA showed excess kurtosis of 15–65; Gaussian bands would be far too narrow on shock days.

09 Model 2 · Regime switching · method

MS-AR / ARX: calm and shock states

Oil alternates between quiet periods and shock periods (slide 3). A Hamilton filter over two latent states lets the mean and volatility change with the state.

Specification Brent fit, Oct 2026
rt = μst + φ rt−1 + δ′xt−1 + σst εt,  st ∈ {calm, shock}
VariantWindowMean weightExogenous x
MS-AR1,260d1.00none
MS-ARX756d0.25other benchmark, DXY, Brent–WTI spread
How a forecast is built
  • Filter today’s state probabilities with fixed parameters (Hamilton filter)
  • Mean: exact regime-weighted cumulative return over h days, × mean weight
  • Band: 4,000 simulated paths through future regimes → ±1σ
  • Refit every 63 trading days; window and weight chosen on 2022–23

daily σ in the shock state vs calm
52 / 14d

Mean weight 0.25 for MS-ARX: validation preferred shrinking the conditional mean three-quarters of the way to no change.

15 Pipeline

From research notebooks to the inbox, every morning

GitHub Actions runs unattended. Models are not refit daily: new data flows through saved models; parameters move quarterly.

Daily · 22:45 UTC (06:45 SGT) daily_update.yml
01 · update_data
Pull last 90 days from Yahoo, FRED, EIA; merge into raw CSVs
→
02 · run_daily
Load saved models, build features, filter regime, forecast μ̂ & σ for h = 1…60
→
03 · commit
Data + append-only forecast_log.csv: an auditable live track record
→
04 · send_email
Signal, forecast table, fan chart, last 7 days vs actual, track record
Quarterly · 1 Jan / Apr / Jul / Oct quarterly_retrain.yml
update_data
same as daily
→
train_models
Re-estimate GARCH, MS-AR and regime parameters, re-select PCA factors, on past data only
→
retrain email
What changed: variables, parameters, old vs new forecast
Why quarterly
Refit every 21 / 63 / 252 days tested on 2016–2026: 63 vs 21 days moved RMSE by ≤ 1.8%, no systematic sign.
Plug-in models
Each family implements train · predict · backtest; the email builds its columns from whatever is in the log.
Monitoring
Frozen evaluation refreshes on the 1st of each month; every forecast is logged before its outcome is known.

19 Conclusions
Oil is moved by shocks no model sees coming. The market prices the rest, so the edge is narrow.
53%
Exogenous shocks: 5% of days carry over half of Brent’s return variance.
≈1.000
Efficient market: no model beats no change on Brent at any horizon.
1 of 8
Economic value: only XGBoost on WTI beats buy & hold (Sharpe 1.16 vs 0.58).
67–76%
Where models help: calibrated uncertainty, not point forecasts.
Limitations. Some design choices were made after seeing post-2024 data. Notebook settings were tuned on Brent and applied to WTI unchanged. Spot prices arrive about a week late, and Brent spot and futures diverged sharply in 2026. XGBoost’s P&L rests on one shock.
